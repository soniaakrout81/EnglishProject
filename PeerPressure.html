Peer Pressure

Description:
Peer pressure is when students feel forced to do things to fit in with friends. This can include cheating, skipping school, trying alcohol or drugs, or joining bullying. Peer pressure affects both learning and behavior, and students may feel unhappy or stressed because of it.

Results:
Studies show students under peer pressure are more likely to use alcohol, feel lonely, or make unsafe choices. Peer pressure can reduce self-confidence, lower academic performance, and create emotional problems. Students may make bad decisions to fit in, which can affect both their studies and personal life.

Solutions:
Schools can teach students to make good decisions, say no when needed, and respect
themselves and others. Encouraging teamwork, kindness, and communication helps students
support each other positively. Parents can talk to their children about peer pressure
and guide them to make safe choices. Reducing peer pressure helps students feel happier,
safer, and more focused on learning.
