Bullying

Description:
Bullying is when students are hurt, teased, or left out by other students. It can happen at school, on the playground, or online. Bullying can be physical, like hitting or pushing; verbal, like name-calling or insulting; or social, like spreading rumors or excluding someone from a group. Cyberbullying happens when students are bullied on social media, messaging apps, or online games. Bullying makes students feel unsafe and unhappy at school.

Results:
About 11% of teenagers report being bullied at school, and 15% say they have
been cyberbullied. Around one-third of teens worldwide experience bullying.
Students who are bullied may feel anxious, depressed, or afraid to go to school.
They may lose confidence, have trouble sleeping, and find it difficult to focus
on learning. In severe cases, bullying can even lead to thoughts of suicide.

Solutions:
To reduce bullying, schools can make strict rules and punishments
for bullying behavior. Teachers and parents should encourage students to
report bullying and provide support to victims. Schools can also run
workshops to teach students about empathy, kindness,
and conflict resolution. Creating a safe and respectful environment helps all students
feel protected and ready to learn.
